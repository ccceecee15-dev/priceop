Requirement 10 · Trigger and entry

Setting EOL is the trigger. The agent's first act is a plan, not a price.

Before

The agent wakes on EOL and proposes this week's markdown. Cycle two proposes the next one. Nobody can see where the event is heading until it is nearly over.

Now

The first run emits the full Hold → MD4 schedule for every store, with expected residual and margin at the exit date. The planner signs off a plan. Later runs report deviation from plan, not deviation from last week.

Plan conflict — decision required

Five review weeks. Five stages. A Hold week costs the slowest stores their MD4.

EOL is set 17 Oct against a hard exit of 14 Nov, and no store may take more than one price change a week. Spending week 1 on Hold leaves four slots, so a store needing the full ladder reaches MD3 and no further. The plan therefore skips Hold for the 61 slowest stores and opens them at MD1 in week 1.

Requirement 11 · Review periodicity

The agent sets the interval. The floor is not negotiable.

One price change per store per week is a store-execution constraint, so it is coded as a floor rather than a setting. The agent may lengthen the interval; the planner may lengthen it further; nobody may go below seven days.

7 days
Agent choice
All five stages reachable
The shortest legal interval, and the only one that gets a slow store all the way to MD4 before the exit date.
10 days
Permitted
MD4 unreachable
Halves the load on store teams but drops a decision point. Proposed only when prior-year variance is low.
14 days
Flagged unsafe
Two stages after Hold
Suits long clearance windows. Against a 14 Nov exit the agent flags it as unsafe for this event.
Requirement 09 · Confidence

Confidence measures the evidence, not the risk

A 65% markdown on a well-observed store can be High confidence. A hold on a store with no recorded sales is Low. That distinction is what makes the bucket useful for routing approvals.

High

Guardrails — the margin stages

Both floors hard

Every stage answers three questions: when it starts, what it prices at, and how low it may go. The numbers are the only thing the planner sets.

MD1
Open the ladder
One depth everywhere, so the price is defensible and the ticket prints in a single pass.
Starts when
7 days after EOL
and only where the store will not clear by 14 Nov at its current rate.
Price
30% off retail
uniform across every eligible store, rounded onto a .49 or .99 ending.
Floor
1.40 × cost
never waived. A store that cannot clear it stays at Hold.
MD2
Differentiate
Depth in proportion to how overstocked the store is — a flat second cut wastes margin.
Starts when
7 days at MD1
and still behind the rate that clears it. Responders hold at MD1.
Price
40 / 50 / 60%
by weeks of cover left: under 6, 6 to 12, or over 12.
Floor
1.30 × cost
and if it leaves under a 15% cut, the store skips to MD3.

The operating envelope

Five rules bind all four stages. No stage setting can widen them, and they are the reason a correct recommendation sometimes shows as "no action this week".

4
Price changes per item per store
Across the whole event, whatever the stages recommend. On a five-week window the calendar binds first.
7
Days minimum between price changes
A store-execution constraint, so it is coded as a floor rather than a setting.
≤5
Units that waive the margin floor
Of the item, in that store. Used by MD3 and MD4 — one definition, both stages.
<50
Or total in-scope units per store
Everything in scope, all styles. Either threshold waives the floor.
off
Automatic execution
Nothing reaches the price file without a named approval. High confidence is bulk-approvable by a person, never automatic.
